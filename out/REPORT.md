### `firebase_content_farm_spam`

**Selector.** `domain LIKE '%.firebaseapp.com' OR domain LIKE '%.web.app'`

**Description.** A large-scale, machine-generated content-farm/SEO-spam network is hosted on randomly-named subdomains of Google's free Firebase hosting (*.firebaseapp.com, *.web.app). Each subdomain name is a Slovak or Scandinavian finance/get-rich-quick keyword (e.g. "akozbohatnut" = how to get rich, "investerarpengar" = Swedish "invest money", "pracovnemiesta" = job vacancies, "danove" = taxes, "nakupzlata" = buy gold) plus a random letter suffix, and each hosts thousands of pages of incoherent, multi-topic text stitched together from scraped fragments (finance/crypto glossary snippets, news snippets, unrelated Q&A, sometimes switching mid-document into Czech, Bosnian/Serbian, or Slovenian). The pages are not authored prose and are not really "Slovak documents" in the intended sense, despite passing the language filter with high average confidence (0.98) and evading near-duplicate dedup entirely (avg minhash_cluster_size ≈ 1, since every domain/page uses a unique random suffix/URL).

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** Domain-level aggregation shows domain LIKE '%.firebaseapp.com' OR '%.web.app' covers 1,408,437 documents (5.32% of the 26.47M-doc corpus), spread across thousands of distinct randomly-suffixed subdomains (e.g. akoinvestovatffsq, akozbohatnuthdiy, pracovnemiestakulp, nakupzlatafvvi, each with ~2,400-2,650 docs, share_of_domain=1.0). Inspected 32 sampled documents (evaluate_detector, seeds 13 and 99): all were incoherent multi-topic scrapes (crypto/finance glossary text abruptly followed by unrelated topics like Simpsons characters, dog food, football results, cat behavior), several with sentences in other Slavic languages (Bosnian/Serbian, Slovenian) interleaved. minhash_cluster_size is ~1 for essentially all of them (avg 1.002) despite the content clearly being templated/recombined across the network, meaning the dedup step entirely misses this network because each page's exact text differs. language_score for the domain averages 0.98, i.e. the langID pipeline is confidently fooled. No counter-examples found among the 32 inspected (no genuine small personal/business Slovak blogs on Firebase hosting turned up in-sample), though it is possible a small minority of genuine sites use this hosting and would be wrongly caught.

**Likely false positives.** A genuine small Slovak personal or hobby site legitimately hosted on Firebase (free Google hosting is popular for small apps/blogs) would be wrongly caught; none were observed in ~32 inspected documents, but the selector cannot distinguish them from the farm by domain pattern alone.

### `adult_spam_farm_wordsalad`

**Selector.** `n_bad_words >= 3`

**Description.** A cluster of throwaway-domain adult/porn spam sites contribute documents that are not coherent Slovak prose but auto-generated word-salad: SEO tag lists, stitched keyword fragments, and repeated boilerplate phrases (e.g. "zadarmo čierne Lesbičky Sex Movies" injected dozens of times) built by a domain-spinning template. The corpus's own language_score is near-perfect (often >0.99) and all three shipped LID tools agree on 'sk', yet the "documents" are not naturally authored Slovak text and provide no usable linguistic signal.

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** Inspected 25 documents matching n_bad_words>=3 across two random samples (seeds 5 and 42): 25/25 were adult-content spam pages from a recognizable network of short-lived, semantically-unrelated domains (adv-moto-girl.bike, accesslocator.{net,org,info}, aagtrades.com, aaronpowell.me, agencerepublique.{me,net}, alvaromd.com.br, baazzaa.com, bananapepper.fr, baddmadrasser.nu, slovhubkia.com, animaliak.net, aemaddin.com, etc.). Text consists of grammatically incoherent fragments and repeated tag/category strings rather than coherent sentences, despite high language_score and LID agreement on 'sk'. domain_stats on this selector shows dozens of domains where 75-92% of ALL their documents match this condition, confirming these are single-purpose spam domains, not occasional profanity in normal content. Counter-check: n_bad_words=1 (excluded by this selector) mostly selects ordinary legitimate content (news, health forums, e-shops, blogs) that merely mentions sex/health topics once or twice, confirming the threshold >=3 is what separates spam farms from normal incidental profanity. Prevalence: 102,474 / 26,470,482 = 0.39% of the full corpus.

**Likely false positives.** Long, legitimate documents that legitimately discuss sex education, health, or relationships in depth and happen to accumulate 3+ profanity-list hits (e.g. a sex-therapy forum thread) rather than being spam-farm content; these appear to be a small minority based on the two 25-document samples, but were not separately quantified.

### `porn_spam_keyword_farm`

**Selector.** `n_bad_words >= 3 AND domain NOT LIKE '%.sk' AND domain NOT LIKE '%.cz'`

**Description.** A second, separate spam network of thousands of randomly-named domains (English dictionary-word-like names + generic gTLDs/ccTLDs such as .com/.me/.fr/.bike/.online/.info/.nl/.it/.us/.br) hosts keyword-stuffed, incoherent "word salad" adult/porn doorway pages that are grammatically broken pastiches of scraped Slovak sentence fragments and tag lists, not genuine documents. Many share a literal CMS template header "Posted by Admin | in <Month> <Day>, 2020 |".

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** domain_stats on n_bad_words>=3 shows dozens of domains (slovhubkia.com, adv-moto-girl.bike, baazzaa.com, aaronpowell.me, alexpichel.com, bananapepper.fr, accesslocator.net/.org/.info, agencerepublique.me/.net, and many more) each contributing 1,000-6,000 docs with 75-92% of their own documents matching high bad-word counts. Inspected 32 documents total via evaluate_detector (seeds 11 and 42): 32/32 were confirmed word-salad porn/dating spam pages — grammatically incoherent strings of scraped fragments/tag lists about explicit content, all with random-looking domain names unrelated to Slovakia, minhash_cluster_size mostly 1 (unique per page despite templated structure). A follow-up sample of the same domains without the bad-word filter (e.g. bananapepper.fr) showed the identical word-salad template style even at n_bad_words=0, meaning the true farm is larger than what this conservative selector catches. The regexp literal 'Posted by Admin \\| in [A-Za-z]+ \\d+, 2020' alone matches an estimated 28,592 docs (0.108%) and also had 12/12 precision when checked independently.

**Likely false positives.** A genuine Slovak site on a non-.sk/.cz domain (e.g. a Slovak business using a .com or .eu domain) that legitimately discusses adult/medical topics or uses informal/profane language (e.g. forum arguments) could be wrongly caught by the bad-word threshold; this is why the domain-diversity check (thousands of distinct low-traffic domains, each ~70-90% flagged) was used as corroborating evidence rather than relying on word count alone.

### `slo_prefix_machine_translated_farm`

**Selector.** `domain LIKE 'slo.%'`

**Description.** A third distinct content-farm network: hundreds of unrelated-looking, seemingly hijacked/repurposed domains (e.g. slo.andhramedicalcollegevizag.com, slo.istanbulmedicine.com, slo.kelinvestgroup.com, slo.orsitaning.ru) all serve the same machine-translated content mirror under a 'slo.' (Slovak) subdomain. Content is overwhelmingly pseudo-medical/health/lifestyle articles whose URL slugs are transliterated Russian (e.g. 'diagnostika-raka-jaichek-u-muzhchin', 'kod-po-mkb-mehanicheskaja-zheltuha'), indicating the same Russian-origin article set has been bulk machine-translated into Slovak (and presumably many other languages via sibling subdomain prefixes) and republished across this domain farm. Unlike the firebaseapp.com spam, the translated text is fluent and grammatically coherent single-topic prose, so it reads as legitimate native Slovak and would not be caught by quality heuristics, but it is not organically authored Slovak content and the same articles are repeated near-verbatim across dozens of domains.

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** 231 distinct domains under the 'slo.' prefix, totaling 73,835 of 26,470,482 documents (0.28% of documents). These documents are far longer than the corpus average (mean 5,060 words vs. corpus-wide mean 438 words, ~11.6x), so they account for a disproportionate ~3.2% of the corpus's total word volume despite being 0.28% of documents. evaluate_detector (seed=6, n=12) inspected 12 sampled documents: all 12 were fluent Slovak health/lifestyle/pet-care articles with Russian-transliterated URL slugs, hosted on domains whose names bear no topical relation to their content (a medical article at 'slo.kelinvestgroup.com', a Nintendo Switch game article at 'slo.gov-civil-lisboa.pt') -- strong evidence of a domain-squatting content-mirror operation rather than genuine publishers. minhash_cluster_size on these documents is mostly 1-6, indicating the near-duplicate detector only partially catches copies of the same underlying article spread across different mirror domains.

**Likely false positives.** A small number of domains under this prefix could coincidentally be legitimate Slovak-prefixed sites unrelated to the farm; none were observed in the 12-document sample, but the prefix match alone is a heuristic, not a certified membership test.

### `porn_doorway_spam_network`

**Selector.** `n_bad_words > 5`

**Description.** A second, distinct content-farm/doorway-page spam network -- unrelated to the firebaseapp/web.app finance farm -- serves auto-generated pornography aggregator pages under ~60+ unrelated-looking domains with generic personal/company names and varied TLDs (adv-moto-girl.bike, baazzaa.com, agencerepublique.net/.me, aaronpowell.me, bananapepper.fr, americanislandsregionalcenter.us, etc.). Pages are keyword-stuffed concatenations of pornographic video-tag phrases machine-translated/rendered into Slovak, not coherent prose, and repeat the same tag vocabulary across thousands of near-identical URLs per domain.

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** n_bad_words > 5 selects 52,354 of 26,470,482 documents (0.20%) across 660 distinct domains. domain_stats shows dozens of domains where 40-65% of ALL that domain's documents match this threshold (e.g. adv-moto-girl.bike 63%, accesslocator.net 64%, badotea.info 63%), indicating whole-domain spam rather than incidental profanity. A follow-up aggregate restricting to domains where >15% of the domain's own documents exceed the threshold (and the domain has >50 docs) found 63 such domains totaling 99,008 documents (0.37% of the corpus) -- i.e. the true footprint of this spam network is roughly double what the bad-words threshold alone catches, since many of its pages use euphemisms/fewer flagged terms per page. I inspected 15 sampled documents via evaluate_detector (seed=11): all 15 (100%) were confirmed pornographic-tag doorway pages with no coherent Slovak prose, matching the pattern (e.g. 'Manelka berie prvy vek penis', 'eben-ghetto-sex', keyword strings like 'Sexy čierna eben sluts sex pre monny'). language_score on these was very high (0.99+) since the individual words/phrases are valid Slovak translations, so the language filter does not catch them.

**Likely false positives.** Legitimate Slovak news/health/forum articles that discuss sex education, sexual health, or crime reporting and happen to use several profanity-list words in a short document (e.g. a rape/abuse news story) could cross the n_bad_words>5 threshold without being spam; none were observed in the 15-document sample but the mechanism is plausible for low-count crossings.

### `czech_slovak_code_switched_docs`

**Selector.** `fasttext='cs' AND langdetect='cs' AND lingua_top='cs'`

**Description.** A systematic subset of documents are Czech-language or heavily Czech/Slovak code-switched, not clean Slovak, yet are labelled language='slk' with high language_score. This mostly comes from (a) Slovak e-commerce sites that paste Czech-language product descriptions/reviews verbatim from shared CZ/SK catalogs and (b) cz.pinterest.com / forum pages whose captions and comments mix Czech and Slovak. Because Czech and Slovak are highly mutually intelligible, the pipeline's own LID and all three shipped auxiliary LIDs (fasttext, langdetect, lingua) confidently but wrongly agree that many such documents are Slovak, and even when they agree the document is Czech, the corpus still ships it labelled slk.

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** Inspected 22 documents where all three auxiliary LIDs (fasttext, langdetect, lingua_top) independently label the text 'cs' while the corpus's own pipeline still labels it 'slk' (seeds 2 and 3). About 16/22 clearly contained substantial or dominant Czech-language text: pure-Czech pages crawled from .cz domains (ciirc.cvut.cz academic page, dg-shop.cz, zks-shop.cz, stoklasa-sk.sk, monelli.cz), and, more frequently, nominally-Slovak e-shops (bonami.sk, svet-kupelne.sk, krasnadoma.sk, b2b.agfoods.sk, kuchynsky-drez.sk) and cz.pinterest.com boards whose product/recipe text is untranslated Czech pasted alongside Slovak site chrome. Counter-evidence: 6/22 were false positives where the text was actually fully idiomatic Slovak (e.g. bezkempu.cz camping listing, modrykonik.sk forum posts, a car-brand name list) that all three external LIDs misjudged as Czech, presumably because Slovak dialectal/colloquial text and short brand-name lists are hard to distinguish from Czech. Prevalence of the strict triple-agreement selector is 44,251/26,470,482 = 0.17% of the full corpus; loosening to fasttext='cs' alone matches 412,528 (1.6%) but with visibly lower precision on manual inspection.

**Likely false positives.** Genuinely Slovak documents (colloquial forum speech, short lists of proper nouns/brand names, or terse product listings) that confuse all three external language identifiers into guessing Czech even though the corpus's own slk label is correct.

### `czech_text_mislabeled_as_slovak`

**Selector.** `fasttext='cs' AND langdetect='cs' AND lingua_top='cs' AND NOT (domain LIKE '%.firebaseapp.com' OR domain LIKE '%.web.app')`

**Description.** A systematic slice of documents are substantially or entirely Czech-language text (product data, forum posts, cookie notices, thesis metadata, Pinterest boards) that the corpus nonetheless labels as Slovak (slk_Latn). These typically come from bilingual CZ/SK commerce platforms, cross-posted forums, and Pinterest country mirrors that reuse Czech copy on .sk domains or vice versa.

**Evidence the auditor gathered.** 44,251 of 26,470,482 documents (0.17%) have all three independent language identifiers (fasttext, langdetect, lingua) agreeing on Czech while the pipeline's own label is Slovak. domain_stats shows this concentrated in identifiable domain classes: Pinterest country mirrors (cz.pinterest.com 9.9% of its own docs, sk.pinterest.com 2.7%), CZ/SK shared e-commerce catalogs (latky-marlen.sk 39%, tenisove-zbozi.cz 51%, sracky.1984.cz 76% -- though the last is actually a nonsense/profanity generator, a separate issue), bilingual parenting/forum sites (modrykonik.sk/.cz), and a Czech thesis repository (dspace.vutbr.cz, 8.7%, though many of these have genuinely Slovak abstracts with only Czech/English metadata labels -- a partial false positive). I inspected 15+15 sampled documents (evaluate_detector seed=9 n=15 and sample_documents seed=3/4): the large majority (~13/15, ~87%) were dominantly or entirely Czech running text (e.g. latky-marlen.sk fabric descriptions in Czech, mandarinka.cz jewelry descriptions in Czech, arome.cz classified ad, audiozone.cz forum reply) with at most a Slovak sentence or two; a minority were genuinely bilingual forum threads (modrykonik) where Slovak and Czech speakers converse in their own language on the same page. I also checked fasttext='cs' alone (without langdetect/lingua agreement): that weaker condition was much noisier, catching many genuinely Slovak pages that fasttext misclassifies on short/quote-heavy text (forum.zdravie.sk, zlavoking.sk, new.rcsolosnica.sk all pure Slovak) -- so I did not use it.

**Likely false positives.** Genuinely bilingual CZ/SK forum threads where the majority language present may still be Slovak in aggregate across the page; Slovak academic abstracts hosted on Czech institutional repositories (dspace.vutbr.cz) where only template/metadata fields are Czech but the abstract body is authentic Slovak.

## Cost and reproducibility

* Classifying every document with an LLM instead, measured on a sample: $3.42 per thousand documents, i.e. $90,456 per pass — against $7.50 for the audit.
* Trajectory replay: 144/147 calls return the same rows (98.0%) and 139 are identical byte for byte in this run. Both counts move by a call or two between repeats, because a query whose ordering key ties at the LIMIT cutoff returns a different tied subset per scan; at most 3 calls differ, and none raises.
* Each validation sample of 25 documents spans at least 18 distinct hosts, largest single host 16%, so the precision intervals are close to the independent-draw case rather than optimistic.
* `scripts/reproduce.sh` runs the whole pipeline.
